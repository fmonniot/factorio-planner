## Bugs


- [ ] **Reactor heat temperature is not in the LP.** Heat counts as an unknown temperature (the key `power:heat@?`, which fits every range), because a reactor's `temperature` is its heat buffer's maximum, not what it settles at under load; a heat exchanger whose minimum the reactor cannot reach is only flagged by the warning, and heat pipe tiers are not considered at all. Needs a model of the reactor's working temperature first.

- [ ] **In-game viewer: leftovers from the planner-connection review (#186).** Three findings were skipped on purpose; none breaks anything today.
  - *Error attribution.* An `error` answers the most recent frame, and after a reconnect the planner sends a restore `snapshot` and an `announce` back to back, so an error for the snapshot looks like one for the announce. The planner forgets the refused bytes after such a pair, which is conservative: it may resend a snapshot that was fine. Fixing it needs the bridge to say which frame an `error` answers (a [protocol](../spec/in-game-viewer-protocol.md) change).
  - *Data-set badge versus `mod.stored`.* The "Data set mismatch" badge comes from the mod's `unknownIds` count in `status`, and is not checked against the plan id the mod reports as stored (`mod.stored`), so the badge can describe a plan other than the one the planner holds. Decide whether the badge should only show when the stored plan is the planner's.
  - *Rebuild debounce.* `src/viewer/viewer.ts` rebuilds the snapshot on every new solver result, with no debounce. Debouncing it would save work while the player edits a big plan. Performance only.
- [ ] **In-game viewer: every beacon configuration.** A row can hold several beacon configurations, but the snapshot sends only the first one that has something to place (`beacon`). Send a `beacons` list instead: the snapshot schema (`src/snapshot/schema.ts`), `npm run contract`, the bridge fixtures, and the mod (`mod/lib/blueprint.lua`, `mod/scripts/cursor.lua`), and every module of a beacon with mixed slots (today only the module most of its slots hold).

## Sourcing priority

Follow-ups of the [sourcing priority initiative](archive/sourcing-priority/plan.md), which shipped.

- [ ] **Connections first with partial supply.** The request probe gives a connections-first item unlimited supply, so when its connections deliver less than the block uses, the own recipes that top it up may need inputs that were not requested (they show as imports). Make the probe aware of the real supply. Left out of [sourcing-priority/](archive/sourcing-priority/plan.md) on purpose (*Receive it first* inherits it); a follow-up to it.
- [ ] **A takes-first take reached before its source gets no credit.** A listed take of a consumer that requests nothing of the item waits for its source and is credited (spec/solver.md, Served order), and a member of a cycle of requests reads the leftover of a member source, but a take of a consumer that also requests the item (a takes-first take) feeds that consumer's requests, so it cannot wait. Three forms remain, all pinned by tests (`src/solver/index.test.ts`, *what a list leaves undone*: the unlisted takes-first consumer that requests from the block of a listed take, and the takes-first take whose source the request order puts after its consumer; case 20 row c1b): (1) a takes-first take whose source the request order puts after its consumer, outside a cycle they share; (2) a takes-first take below a take not credited yet, including an unnamed takes-first consumer that requests from the block of a listed take; (3) inside a cycle, a member's take below an outside take not credited yet, and a take that reads a back estimate in the final round of a cycle that does not settle. In each the take is credited 0, flagged `notCredited`, and its source's surplus stays unused unless another consumer takes it. Possible fix: iterate the request pass (credit, then redo the consumers' requests) until the credits settle, which may not converge.
- [ ] **Served order: what the list-order fallback leaves out.** A group of takes is shared by need when the planner can learn every member's need first; otherwise it is served in list order and the list shows a badge. The fallback also covers cases a narrower rule could share: (a) a group of takes on a pair any of whose consumers is in a cycle of requests, even when no member of the group is (a "member in the cycle" rule would be safe; sharing inside the demand rounds is the cut); (b) a group with a member whose requester is reached after the first member; (c) a group reached before its source, or before a take or a group above it; (d) a member that takes the item from another source earlier in its connection order; (e) a block with area entries in more than one group, or in a group and alone. Also: an area member's credit is split by need, not by its lines' weights. Source: the T4 report in [sourcing-priority/phase-4.md](archive/sourcing-priority/phase-4.md#t4-report).
- [ ] **Served order: a take's credit at its block's first entry.** A take is credited once per connection at its block's first entry, so a take listed between two entries of one block is credited as if the whole block sat at the first one (J gets 10 where a rank-faithful solve gives 22), and when a consumer's first entry is for an area that needs nothing, a take ranked below it loses credit. Fixing both needs per-entry crediting with lookahead for lists without `tied`, which breaks the bit-identity of lists that predate groups. Pinned by the tests named "limitation" (case 19 j2 and j4 in `src/solver/synthetic.test.ts`); see the T3 and T4 reports in [sourcing-priority/phase-4.md](archive/sourcing-priority/phase-4.md#t3-report).
- [ ] **Served order: a stale member of a mixed group shifts the ratios.** A member with no `share` reads as the mean of the stored ones, so dropping a stale member of a mixed group changes the ratios of the rest (`{B 80, C 20, D}`: C and D are 20:50, and 50:50 once B goes). It follows from the rule as written (spec/solver.md, Served order). Decide whether a stale member should keep its weight until the list is edited. Source: the T2 report in [sourcing-priority/phase-4.md](archive/sourcing-priority/phase-4.md#t2-report).
- [ ] **Served order: expanding a plain entry that sits in a group.** The first edit of a list writes a line per area for a plain consumer entry, and a plain entry inside a group of two or more becomes several members that store equal shares, so the figures of a short group can change although the edit did not touch it. Keep such an entry one member with an inner split, or expand only the entry the gesture names. Two smaller findings of the T6 review: `FactorySummary` and `BlockGraph` call `splitServed` without the zero-need predicate the popover passes, and `splitServed` calls `linesOf` twice per stored entry (a Map keyed by the entry would do). Source: the T6 report in [sourcing-priority/phase-4.md](archive/sourcing-priority/phase-4.md#t6-report).
- [ ] **Area rules: reserving a byproduct for the area with the rule.** *Don't make more here* on an area does not reserve the block's byproduct for it: the LP sends it where it saves the most machines, so the other consumer has to switch off the area that makes it (D8). No single LP expresses the reservation. Source: [sourcing-priority/phase-5.md](archive/sourcing-priority/phase-5.md#a-byproduct-goes-where-it-saves-machines).
- [ ] **Areas matrix: connected and imported in the outside column.** For items no area rule touches, the matrix's outside column does not split connected supply from import; the split exists only where the LP has per-area slacks and supplies. Source: [sourcing-priority/phase-5.md](archive/sourcing-priority/phase-5.md).
- [ ] **Area rules: a short offer between a *Receive it first* area and a higher area.** When the offer is below what the *Receive it first* area and a higher area with a residual both ask for, pass 2 gives it in table order. Capping the supplies of the other areas at what the first areas leave of the offer would follow the rule more closely. Source: the T4 report in [sourcing-priority/phase-5.md](archive/sourcing-priority/phase-5.md#t4-report).
- [ ] **Area rules: an area's sentence and the group panel read whole-block figures.** An area's sentence names the block's connections, not the ones that reach that area, and the group panel's flow lines list net flows only, so a relay through an area shows as net. Both would read the area's own routes (`areaRoutes`). Source: the T5 and T6 reports in [sourcing-priority/phase-5.md](archive/sourcing-priority/phase-5.md#t5-report).
- [ ] **Area LP: a pair item's block-level `received` can disagree with its area legs.** Case 22 C with S pinned in a third area N and M's rule switching N off for the ingot: the outside leg into M says 50 supplied, the block's `received` says 40. Found while writing the T3 tests of the sourcing-priority phase 6; not caused by the connection order. Find which of the two is right and align the bookkeeping.

## Surplus rule

Follow-ups of the [surplus rule initiative](archive/surplus-rule/plan.md), which shipped (the Leftover switch, solver and player-facing).

- [ ] **The connection order for a leftover.** A loop that needs an input a connection offers stays open under the default rule: pass 1 prices that input as an import and the residual leaves no room for the loop, so the player must set the input to *Receive it first* (synthetic case 28, rows e and f). Open the gate of `needsConnectionOrder` also when an item with Leftover off is still left over and a default-rule connection has offer left, so that the least-received step, which carries the price, takes the input the loop needs.
- [ ] **A Leftover rule per area.** The rule is the block's alone; at area scope the control shows it read-only. Add an area-level rule if a plan needs an area that loops its own item while the block's other areas may leave it over.

## Planning features

- [ ] **Mining productivity research.** Extraction rows use the drill's base mining speed. Mining productivity research and a drill's base `effect_receiver` are ignored.
- [ ] **Wind turbines and other `electric-energy-interface` generators.** Seablock's `wind-turbine-2` (15 kW) and Nullius' wind turbines and Stirling engines are `electric-energy-interface` entities whose output is set by script (wind, altitude, heat). Decide an average per entity and export them as generation recipes.
- [ ] **Burnt results of fuels.** A fuel with a `burnt_result` is never offered. Model the burnt item as an output of the burner row.
- [ ] **Fluid-burning non-crafting machines.** Drills and pumps with a `burns_fluid` source are exported as `fluid-burner` but have no UI or tests.
- [ ] **Heat-powered crafting machines.** Crafting machines with a heat energy source are not tied to the `power:heat` item.
- [ ] **Fusion and Space Age generators.** Fusion reactors and generators, lightning attractors and per-surface solar power are not exported; neither bundle has them.
- [ ] **Beacon power.** Beacon energy is not counted in a row's power (spec/solver.md); needed for self-powering blocks.
- [ ] **Optimization mode.** Minimize machines or power through the LP objective. Two real plans show the objective matters more than the unit: the solver leaves rows at zero and lets an unwanted output pile up. The options (machines, priced surplus, required rows) and the measurements are in [surplus-aware-objective/](surplus-aware-objective/plan.md).
- [ ] **Module warnings: recipe-level misfits and per-slot rings.** `module-not-allowed` judges the machine or beacon only. A module the recipe refuses (its limitation lists, or productivity on a recipe that disallows it) is not warned about, and the Machine section names the misfit modules in one message instead of ringing the slot that holds each.
